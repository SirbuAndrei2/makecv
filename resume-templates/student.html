Student Resume Template for Your First Job

By the MakeCV team · Updated October 27, 2026

When you are a student or recent graduate, your degree and what you have built are your best evidence, not a long list of jobs. MakeCV's Student template is built around that: Education first, Projects second, then Experience, in a single clean column with a calm teal accent. It works for internships, part-time jobs, graduate schemes and first full-time roles.

Why the section order matters

Recruiters skim the top third of a page first. On a typical student resume that space is better spent on your course and your projects than on a weekend job. The Student template orders sections for that reader:

  1. Profile: two or three sentences on what you study, one result you are proud of, and the role you want.
  2. Education: degree or school, dates (use the expected year if you have not finished), and a notes line for grades, honours or relevant modules.
  3. Projects: coursework, hackathons, societies, research or personal projects, with the tools you used and a link if you have one.
  4. Experience: part-time work, internships, volunteering and informal work. All of it counts.
  5. Skills, then Languages and Certifications side by side to save space.

What to write when you have little experience

  • Turn coursework into projects. "Built a budgeting app in a team of four for a software engineering module; presented it to 40 classmates" is a project with a role and a result.
  • Describe informal work like a job. Tutoring, babysitting, helping in a family business or running a small online shop show reliability and responsibility.
  • Write one achievement per line. Each line becomes a bullet. Start with a verb and add a number where you honestly can.
  • Pick skills that match the advert. Six to ten is plenty; see skills for your resume.

The full guide to writing a resume with no experience has examples for each section.

Student vs Harvard

Both put Education first and both are single-column and ATS-safe. Harvard is strictly black and white with centred headings, the traditional choice for finance, consulting and law. Student adds a small amount of colour, a photo option for countries where photos are expected, and puts Projects above Experience, which suits technical, creative and early-career applicants better.

Photo or no photo?

Leave it out for applications in the US, Canada, the UK and Ireland. In parts of continental Europe a photo is still common. If you don't upload one, the template simply has no photo area: nothing empty is left on the page.

Frequently asked questions

What should a student resume include?
Contact details, a two- or three-sentence profile, education (with relevant courses or grades if they help), projects, any work or volunteering, skills, and languages. One full page is enough.
Is the Student template good for high school students?
Yes. Put your school in Education with your expected graduation year, then add projects, volunteering and any informal work such as tutoring or babysitting. The guide to writing a resume with no experience has examples.
Is the Student template ATS-friendly?
Yes. It is a single column with standard headings. Only the Languages and Certifications lists sit side by side at the bottom, and they are short. Use the ATS PDF button for online applications.
Can I move Experience above Education later?
Switch templates instead: your details stay the same, and Classic, Compact or Professional put Experience first once your work history is your strongest section.
