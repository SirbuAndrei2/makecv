Free CV Template: UK Format and What to Include

By the MakeCV team · Updated October 27, 2026

In the UK and Ireland, a CV (curriculum vitae) is the document you send for almost every job: usually two sides of A4, tailored to the role, with no photo. This page shows a UK-style layout you can fill in for free, explains each section, and covers the conventions that differ from an American resume.

UK CV format at a glance

ConventionUK and Ireland
Length2 pages of A4 (1 page for school leavers and many graduates)
PhotoNo
Date of birth, marital status, nationalityNo (state your right to work if relevant)
Opening paragraphPersonal statement, 3–4 sentences
OrderReverse chronological: most recent job first
SpellingBritish English (organised, programme, licence)
ReferencesNot on the CV; provided when asked
FilePDF or Word, as the advert asks

Sections to include, in order

1. Name and contact details

Full name as the heading (not "Curriculum Vitae"), then phone, email, town or city, and LinkedIn if it is current. A full postal address is no longer necessary.

2. Personal statement

Three or four sentences answering: who are you, what can you offer this employer, and what are you looking for? A dependable formula:

Formula

[Role or identity] with [X years / relevant background] in [area]. [One strong, specific achievement]. [Key skills that match the advert]. Looking for [type of role] where I can [contribution].

Example: experienced

CIPD-qualified HR advisor with six years in retail and logistics. Rolled out a new absence policy across 14 sites that reduced short-term absence by a fifth in its first year. Experienced in employee relations casework, TUPE transfers and HR systems. Looking for a senior advisor role in a growing multi-site business.

Example: graduate

Recent Geography graduate (2:1, University of Leeds) with strong data and mapping skills from a dissertation using QGIS and census data. Organised a 40-volunteer campus recycling scheme. Looking for a graduate role in environmental consultancy.

(Fictional examples.) More in our summary and personal statement examples.

3. Work experience

Job title, employer, location and dates (month and year), most recent first. Under each, two to five lines of achievements, not duties. Start with a verb, add numbers where honest. Explain gaps briefly if you want to ("Career break: caring responsibilities").

4. Education

Degree, institution and year; classification (2:1, First) if recent and helpful. A-levels or Leaving Certificate results matter mainly for school leavers and graduates; experienced candidates can summarise them in a line or leave them out.

5. Skills

Software, languages, licences (full UK driving licence, if the role involves driving) and job-specific skills. See how to choose skills.

6. Optional sections

Projects, certifications (CIPD, ACCA, PRINCE2, CSCS card…), volunteering and languages. Hobbies only if they say something relevant about you.

The UK government's National Careers Service has a good overview of CV sections if you want a second opinion.

Five things that make a UK CV look American (and how to fix them)

  1. A photo: remove it.
  2. US spelling: "organized", "program", "color". Switch to British English.
  3. "Resume" in the file name or heading: call it a CV.
  4. US Letter paper: use A4 (MakeCV already does).
  5. GPA: use your degree classification instead.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a UK CV be?
Two sides of A4 is the usual guide. School leavers and graduates often fit on one page; very senior candidates sometimes need three, but two is the norm.
Should I put a photo on my CV in the UK?
No. UK employers generally do not expect a photo, and leaving it out avoids any question of bias. The same applies in Ireland.
Do I need to include references on my CV?
No. Employers ask for referees later in the process. You don't need to write "references available on request" either; it is assumed.
What is a personal statement on a CV?
It is the short paragraph at the top of a UK CV (called a professional summary in the US): three or four sentences on who you are, what you offer and what you are looking for.
