Skills to Put on a Resume (and How to Choose Them)

By the MakeCV team · Updated October 27, 2026

The skills section is a quick index of what you can do, and for applicant tracking systems it is often where keyword searches find you. The goal isn't to list everything you have ever touched; it is to list the skills this job needs that you can back up.

Hard skills vs soft skills

Hard skillsSoft skills
What they areTeachable, testable abilities: tools, techniques, languages, certificationsHow you work: communication, leadership, problem-solving
ExamplesExcel, SQL, bookkeeping, forklift licence, Adobe Premiere, phlebotomyNegotiation, coaching, stakeholder management, conflict resolution
Where to show themSkills section and experienceMainly in experience achievements and your summary

How to choose your skills in 4 steps

  1. Collect the advert's skills. Highlight every tool, technique and qualification in the job advert.
  2. Keep only what you can prove. For each, ask: could I talk about a time I used this?
  3. Use the advert's wording. If it says "Microsoft Excel", don't write "spreadsheets". If it says "CRM (Salesforce)", write "Salesforce CRM".
  4. Order by relevance. The skills the advert mentions first or most often go first.

In MakeCV, type a skill and press Enter or comma to add it. Each skill has a three-step level selector, which some templates show as bars or dots. The skill name is what recruiters and parsers read, so word it carefully.

Proving skills beyond the list

A skill that appears both in your skills section and in an achievement is far more convincing:

Example

Skills: Excel, Power BI
Experience: Built a Power BI dashboard from three Excel trackers, saving the operations team about four hours of reporting a week.

Common mistakes

  • Listing basics such as "email" or "internet". They are assumed.
  • Listing everything, which buries the skills that matter.
  • Skills you can't discuss, which an interviewer will test.
  • Relying on bars or percentages to show level. Write it in words if it matters.
  • Not updating the list per job, so the order no longer matches the advert.

Frequently asked questions

How many skills should I put on my resume?
Usually six to twelve. Enough to cover the main requirements of the job advert, few enough that each one is believable.
Should I include soft skills?
Include one or two if the advert stresses them, but prove them in your experience instead of only listing them. "Resolved escalated complaints for a 40-store region" shows communication better than the word "communication".
Should I rate my skill levels?
Bars, stars and percentages are decoration: recruiters can't interpret them and parsers ignore them. If a level matters (languages, certain software), write it in words: "Spanish (C1)", "Excel (advanced: pivot tables, Power Query)".
Where should the skills section go?
After experience and education for most people, or near the top for technical roles and career changers. Some templates place skills in a side column; the content matters more than the position.
