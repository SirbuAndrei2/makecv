Quick comparison

"ATS" below is our own assessment of how safely a parser can read the layout. Best means one reading column and standard headings; Good means a narrow side column with plain headings; Fair means a sidebar, cards or a dark page, which look great to a human reader but carry more parsing risk.

"Optional" photo means the template shows your photo if you upload one. If you skip it, nothing is left in its place: no empty frame or placeholder icon. Harvard, Compact and Minimalist have no photo area at all.

Templates by style

Each style guide shows the templates in that family at full size and explains when the style helps and when it hurts:

  • Professional resume template: a clean two-column corporate layout with an optional photo.
  • Harvard resume template: the traditional black-and-white format with Education first.
  • Student resume template: Education and Projects first, for first jobs and internships.
  • Simple resume template: plain, single-column, works for almost any job (Classic and Compact).
  • Modern resume template: sidebar and two-column layouts with colour.
  • Minimalist resume template: black-and-white, typography-led designs.
  • Creative resume template: bold colour and cards for creative fields.
  • Executive resume template: understated serif design for senior roles.
  • Tech resume template: dark, developer-style layouts for engineering roles.

How to choose in 30 seconds

  1. Applying through an online portal (Workday, Greenhouse, Taleo, iCIMS and similar)? Pick Harvard, Classic or Minimalist and download the ATS PDF.
  2. Senior role, conservative industry (law, finance, government)? Executive or Classic.
  3. Emailing a person directly, or a design/creative role? Any template works; Modern, Aurora, Creative and Bloom stand out most.
  4. Student or first job? Student puts your education and projects first; Harvard is the more traditional option.
  5. Lots of experience to fit on one page? Compact.
  6. Developer or data role? Tech / Dark reads well on screen; keep Classic as a second version for strict portals.
  7. Applying in the UK or Ireland? Skip the photo (see the UK CV guide). In parts of continental Europe a photo is still common.

Because your details are typed in once, it costs nothing to keep two versions: a design-forward PDF for people and a plain one for portals.
