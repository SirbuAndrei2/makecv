Resume Summary Examples (and How to Write Yours)

By the MakeCV team · Updated October 27, 2026

The professional summary is the short paragraph at the top of your resume (called a personal statement on UK CVs). It is often the only part read in full on the first pass, so it should say who you are, prove it with one result, and point at the job you want. Below: a formula, then 20 examples you can adapt.

All examples are fictional and written for illustration. Replace every number with your own real figures.

The 4-part formula

  1. Identity: your role and experience level ("Registered nurse with 4 years in acute medicine").
  2. Proof: one specific, measurable achievement.
  3. Fit: two or three skills from the job advert.
  4. Direction (optional): the role you want next.

Aim for two to four sentences. In MakeCV the summary box suggests 300–500 characters and counts as you type.

Early career

1. Student with no work experience

Second-year business student at the University of Manchester with strong spreadsheet and presentation skills. Led a five-person team that won the faculty's case competition with a pricing plan for a local gym. Looking for a summer internship in operations or analytics.

2. High-school student, first job

Reliable high-school junior with a perfect attendance record and two years of volunteering at a community food bank, sorting donations and serving up to 150 families each Saturday. Friendly, punctual and available on weekends.

3. Recent graduate

Computer science graduate (2026) with internship experience building internal tools in Python and React. Shipped a dashboard used daily by a 30-person support team. Looking for a junior full-stack developer role.

4. Apprentice or trades starter

Level 2 electrical apprentice with 18 months of hands-on domestic installation work and an excellent safety record. Comfortable reading drawings, testing circuits and dealing with customers on site.

Career changers and returners

5. Teacher to instructional design

Secondary-school science teacher of 7 years moving into instructional design. Built a flipped-classroom course used by 9 colleagues that raised end-of-year pass rates in my classes from 68% to 81%. Skilled in learning objectives, assessment design and Articulate Rise.

6. Hospitality to customer success

Restaurant manager with 6 years of leading teams of up to 20 and resolving guest complaints on the spot. Raised review scores from 4.1 to 4.6 by retraining front-of-house staff. Moving into customer success for a SaaS company.

7. Returning after a career break

Payroll administrator with 8 years' experience, returning after a three-year career break for family care. Recently completed CIPP payroll update training. Accurate, deadline-driven and experienced with Sage and Xero.

8. Military to civilian

Former army logistics sergeant with 10 years of planning supply operations for units of up to 600 people. Managed inventory worth over $4M with zero audit findings. Seeking a supply chain or operations management role.

Office, sales and service

9. Customer service representative

Customer service representative with 3 years in a high-volume telecom contact centre, handling 60+ calls a day with a 94% satisfaction score. Calm with escalations, quick on CRM systems, and fluent in English and Spanish.

10. Administrative assistant

Administrative assistant supporting a 12-person leadership team. Introduced a shared calendar and booking process that ended double-bookings of meeting rooms. Advanced in Microsoft 365 and comfortable handling confidential information.

11. Sales representative

B2B sales representative with 4 years selling software to small businesses. Closed 118% of quota in 2025 and built the territory's largest referral pipeline. Strong at discovery calls, demos and CRM discipline in HubSpot.

12. Retail assistant

Retail sales assistant with 2 years at a busy high-street fashion store. Regularly top three for add-on sales and trusted with opening, closing and cash reconciliation. Looking for a supervisor role.

Professional and technical

13. Software engineer

Backend engineer with 6 years building payment systems in Go and PostgreSQL. Led the migration to an event-driven architecture that cut failed transactions by 70%. Comfortable owning services end to end, from design to on-call.

14. Data analyst

Data analyst with 3 years in e-commerce, fluent in SQL, Python and Looker. Built the weekly retention model that the marketing team now uses to plan campaigns. Looking to grow into an analytics engineering role.

15. Accountant

ACCA-qualified accountant with 6 years in mid-sized manufacturing. Cut month-end close from 8 to 5 working days by automating reconciliations. Experienced with IFRS reporting, audits and SAP.

16. Marketing manager

Product marketing manager with 7 years launching B2B software. Led go-to-market for 12 releases and grew qualified pipeline by 38% in two years. Strong at positioning, sales enablement and customer research.

17. Registered nurse

Registered nurse with 4 years on an acute medical ward of 28 beds. Mentored 9 student nurses and led the ward's falls-prevention audit. Calm under pressure, IV-trained, and experienced with electronic patient records.

18. Project manager

PRINCE2-certified project manager with 8 years delivering IT and facilities projects worth up to £3M. Brought a delayed office relocation back on schedule and 6% under budget. Skilled in stakeholder reporting and risk management.

Senior and leadership

19. Operations director

Operations director with 15 years in third-party logistics. Run a 9-site network of 1,200 people with a €60M budget; consolidated three warehouse systems into one and cut order-to-dispatch time by 30%. Known for turnarounds and calm change management.

20. Head of engineering

Engineering leader who has grown teams from 8 to 45 engineers across three product lines. Introduced a platform team and on-call practices that halved incident recovery time. Looking for a VP Engineering role at a growth-stage company.

Five mistakes to avoid

  1. Adjective stacks: "dynamic, passionate, results-driven". Replace them with one result.
  2. Writing about what you want only: "Seeking an opportunity to grow…". Lead with what you offer.
  3. Too long: over five lines and it stops being read.
  4. Generic for every job: change the title and skills to match each advert.
  5. Numbers you can't explain: every figure is a likely interview question.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a resume summary be?
Two to four sentences, roughly 300–500 characters. That is about three lines on the page, short enough to read in a few seconds.
Is a resume summary the same as an objective?
Not quite. An objective says what you want; a summary says what you offer. Modern resumes use a summary, sometimes ending with one line about the role you are looking for.
Should I write my summary in the first person?
Resume convention drops the "I": "Accountant with six years…" rather than "I am an accountant with six years…". Either is acceptable as long as you are consistent.
Can I use the same summary for every job?
Keep the same core, but change the job title and the skills you mention to match each advert. It takes a minute and makes your resume look written for that role.
